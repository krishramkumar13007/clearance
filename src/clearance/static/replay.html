Give permission once. Your assistant knows when to ask again.

Clearance sits between an AI assistant and your email. You approve a standing permission one time; routine follow-ups then go out quietly, and only a draft that crosses the line comes back to you.

An assistant that checks every email with you asks every single time. By the twentieth "yes" you stop reading, and the one that matters slips through.

1 in 16

On 34 test emails, Clearance asked about 1 in 16 routine ones and caught all 18 that needed you: a promised deadline, a quoted price, a new recipient.

  1. One permission card. The assistant asks to send routine follow-ups on a pilot project to four people. Approve it.
  2. Quiet sends. Notes, status and scope replies go out without asking you.
  3. One exception. A draft promises a Monday go-live. Clearance stops it and says why. Your call.

    Not just email

    Approval fatigue shows up wherever an assistant acts for you. Asking about everything trains people to click yes; asking about nothing is how mistakes go out. Clearance handles email and coding agents today, and the same shape fits the rest: one permission with clear edges, quiet work inside it, and a card only at the edge.

    • Email and messages
    • Calendar invites
    • Purchases and payments
    • Sharing files
    • Forms and sign-ups
    • Coding agents

    Add it to your assistant

    Clearance is a layer between the assistant and the tools that act. The assistant asks Clearance for a permission once, then sends every action through it: inside the permission, it runs; at the edge, you get one card that says why. It plugs in as an MCP server (clearance-mcp) or over plain HTTP, so an assistant only has to route its actions through it.
